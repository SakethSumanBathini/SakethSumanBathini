Problem statement (final, 60 words)

When a university exam paper leaks on WhatsApp, nobody can tell which of its hundreds of colleges leaked it. Leakers retype a few questions, stripping QR codes and printed IDs, so sources are found, if at all, by luck or police work, and every student risks a re-exam. It happened at Kannur University and Karnataka State Law University in 2025.

Students

Lose an exam they prepared for and sit a re-exam, punished for one college's leak.

Universities

Pay for re-exams and observers in every college, and lose credibility each time.

Investigators

Start with no lead and spend weeks or months on phone forensics.

Every current fix breaks when someone retypes five questions.

Giving each recipient a slightly different copy is an old idea: spies call it a canary trap, and IBM patented a version for exam papers in 2003. What nobody has deployed is a version that survives retyping across hundreds of colleges and states its own error rate.

Same questions, same marks. A different pattern for every college.

Each college's paper puts the questions in its own order and uses its own approved wording, like “Compare a stack and a queue” or “Compare a queue and a stack”. Marks, content and difficulty stay the same. That pattern is the college's code, and it survives retyping.

Read the small grids like an OMR sheet. Columns A to J are the ten questions every college gets, rows are where each one is printed, and the number in a filled bubble is the wording that college got. Point at or tap a question to compare.

      “Names the source from” is the smallest leak named in at least 95% of cases. Before we hardened the decoder on 18 Oct, the same 148,000 leaks named 8 innocent colleges. Three changes, plus a bug fix in the second check: numbers that simply run 1, 2, 3 may be the leaker’s own, so both readings are weighed; a line’s wording counts only when it clearly matches one approved wording; and the bar went from 1,000 to 10,000 to 1.

      Only one step of today's delivery changes.

      Universities already send each college a PDF. Leak Trace sits at the moment those PDFs are made, and again when a leak appears.

      Before the exam

      1. 1Setter writes one paper

        And approves four meaning-neutral wordings per question.

      2. 2Codebook makes 500 versions

        One per college, kept secret by the controller of exams.

      3. 3Per-college PDFs and keys

        Each paper prints its version ID for the valuers.

      4. 4Existing portal delivers

        OTP or PIN as today; each college prints its own copy.

      After a leak

      1. 5Leak spotted

        A phone photo, or 2–10 questions retyped on WhatsApp.

      2. 6Read it

        OCR reads photos; fuzzy matching ties typo-ridden lines to the master questions.

      3. 7Compare with all 500

        A likelihood for every college; a second stage checks for mixed copies.

      4. 8Ranked lead

        Names a college only when it is 10,000 times likelier than any other; otherwise a shortlist.

      How it answers the six judging criteria.

      Problem clarity
      Real, recent leaks at Kannur, KSLU and the Karnataka SSLC exams. A survey of our own students adds first-hand evidence.
      Originality
      Builds openly on the canary trap and IBM's 2003 patent. New: survives retyped, partial leaks across 500 colleges and states its error rate.
      Demonstrated feasibility
      Working simulation, OCR test, an attack test of 148,000 leaks and the live demo on this page. A leak lab with classmates on 21–22 Oct.
      Cost
      Software only. It changes one step, making each college's PDF, and runs on a laptop.
      Beneficiary impact
      A re-exam at one college instead of all. Kannur's sub-committee recommended exactly that once the source was known.
      Scalability
      The same method serves state boards and paper-delivery vendors that reach 2,000+ colleges; more colleges need about one more leaked line, because the bar rises with their number.

      Different order, same exam.

      • We reorder only where order does not change difficulty: equal-mark short questions, either/or blocks, MCQ options and sub-parts.
      • Related questions stay together and the easy-to-hard order stays fixed.
      • A faculty member approves every wording variant.
      • Average marks for each version are published after the exam.

      What it cannot do yet.

      • All results so far are simulated. The leak lab adds the first real data.
      • English only for now. OCR for Telugu, Kannada, Tamil and Malayalam is untested.
      • A leaker who rewords every question or shuffles the lines can stop it naming anyone. In our tests it then gave a shortlist instead of blaming an innocent college. A trace is a lead to confirm, not proof.
      • Two colleges mixing their copies can, rarely, make a third look guilty: twice in 65,000 simulated mixes at our old 1,000-to-1 bar, none in 45,000 at 10,000 to 1. A structured codebook is the next fix.
      • Leaks from the setter or the press happen before versions exist, so they cannot be traced to a college.
